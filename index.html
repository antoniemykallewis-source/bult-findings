01 / The findings

Trust comes first.

The goal we framed this around: a steady 10,000 active subscribers. Not 10k new a month. That makes this mostly a retention game, so most of these are about trust and keeping people past month two.

01 / Refund policy

The refund policy argues with the guarantee.

What we saw

The product page says "30 days, no question asked, keep the pouches, no return shipping," but the refund policy asks for unused items in original packaging, says "[INSERT RETURN ADDRESS]," and points late refunds to a Gmail address.

Why it matters for subs

The guarantee is what gets a skeptical guy to start month one. If he reads the policy and it says the opposite, he either doesn't start or doesn't trust you when it's time to renew.

The fix

Rewrite the refund policy to match the guarantee word for word, fill in the address, and swap the Gmail for support@bult.com.

02 / Terms of service

Terms of Service is still the Shopify template.

What we saw

The Terms page still has template blanks like "[LINK]" and calls the store "getbult."

Why it matters for subs

Subscription buyers check the fine print before giving a card for recurring billing. Blanks read as "nobody's home."

The fix

Fill every placeholder, use the real business name, and have counsel give it one pass.

03 / Science links

Some Science page links go to the wrong papers.

What we saw

At least 6 of the 28 "View Study" links open unrelated research: B6 goes to a coffee review, Folate to acupuncture and IVF, B12 to an EPA depression trial, Copper to amino acids and obesity, Chromium to carotenoids in newborns, and Muira Puama to "The Full Monty and life-long learning."

Why it matters for subs

The science page is the brand's main reason to believe. A buyer who checks the evidence may notice; that can undermine confidence in staying.

The fix

Audit all 28 PMIDs against the ingredient, fix the links, and add a "last reviewed" date.

04 / Buy once flow

"Buy once" sends you back to the subscription.

What we saw

The "Buy once" button at the bottom of the product page jumps back to the top of the page, where the subscription option is still the one selected.

Why it matters for subs

We didn't test checkout, and we're not saying it breaks. But a one-time buyer who feels nudged into a subscription is the one who cancels fast and complains. Happy one-time buyers convert to subs later.

The fix

Make "Buy once" select the one-time option (or go straight to cart with it), and show which option is active right on the button.

05 / Results timeline

Homepage and product page tell different timelines.

What we saw

The homepage "90 Day Foundation" says days 1-30, 30-60, 60-90+. The product page says days 1-45, 45-90, 90+.

Why it matters for subs

This timeline is basically the retention promise. It tells people when to expect results, which is when they decide to cancel or stay.

The fix

Pick one timeline and use it everywhere, including in the post-purchase emails.

06 / Blend transparency

"No proprietary blends" has an exception.

What we saw

The product page says "No proprietary blends. No hidden doses," while FAQ #24 says the Prebiotic and Probiotic Blend "is the one exception."

Why it matters for subs

Transparency is the pitch. A small exception is fine. Finding it buried in the FAQ feels like a gotcha.

The fix

Put the exception next to the claim, like "No proprietary blends, except our probiotic, and here's every strain."

07 / Welcome kit

The welcome kit is a secret.

What we saw

The FAQ has questions about a Welcome Kit and an aluminum dispenser, but the subscription offer box never mentions either one.

Why it matters for subs

If the kit is currently included, making that clear could give buyers another reason to pick subscribe over buy once. Right now the offer box does not explain it.

The fix

Confirm current kit availability and eligibility first. If included, add a clear line and photo to the subscription box, with any conditions.

08 / Pricing story

The price has moved since the press releases.

What we saw

The Apr 21 press release says $100 one-time or $90 on subscription. The Oct 14 release says $90 a month. The site today says $112 one-time and $89 every 30 days.

Why it matters for subs

People who found BULT through press may expect the older price. These are dated historical prices, not evidence of a broken live price. The one-time price difference is $12.

The fix

Keep one clear current price table on the site. Where the publisher allows it, add a dated update to older releases.

09 / Meta ads

The visible Meta ads are new.

What we saw

Meta Ad Library shows about 24 results for the active-US exact-phrase search "bult.com." The six visible Bult ad cards started Oct 20 or Oct 23, 2026. This is a keyword-search snapshot, not a complete advertiser inventory.

Why it matters for subs

The visible ads are recent. It also means the fixes above are landing in front of paid traffic right now; this public snapshot cannot tell us which ads bring people who stay.

The fix

Tag every ad so you can tell which ones bring people who stick past month two, not just who buys first.

Onnit Total Human.

The offer + subscription

Total Human runs $137.95 one-time or $117.26 on "Subscribe & Save 15%," in 30, 60 or 90 count supplies. The page also shows a 30-day money back guarantee, "HSA/FSA eligible," a quiz banner up top, and a 10% off email popup.

Their ads hammer one simple idea: "No more clunky bottles... two simple packs per day." About 37 active-US keyword-search results for "onnit.com"; the visible Onnit ads mostly started Oct 7. How BULT beats it

BULT already owns the same "packs not bottles" story, and it's one pouch, not two. Say that out loud. Show the subscription savings as a clear % the way Onnit does, and build reviews hard, since social proof is where Onnit's way ahead.

11 / Competitive context

Persona Nutrition.

The offer + subscription

The banner says "2 Ways to Save: Save 20% or Buy 2, Get 1 Free." The hero pushes a 5-minute quiz and "Talk to a nutritionist," says packs are "built around your meds with 4,000+ interactions checked," and links "10k+ reviews on Trustpilot."

The play worth borrowing

Human help as the hook. Real nutritionists with names and faces. Their ads name the real reason people quit: "It's not a willpower problem. It's a friction problem... the bottles just sit there." About 19 active-US keyword-search results for "personanutrition.com," not a complete advertiser count. Trustpilot shows 10,208 reviews at 4.4.

How BULT beats it

BULT already has a urologist and a registered dietitian quoted in its own press release. Make those existing expert voices more prominent. An "ask our dietitian" option could be worth exploring if expert availability and consent support it. Borrow the friction angle in ads and emails, since one pouch a day is the answer to it. Keep it simple. No quiz needed when it's one product.

12 / Competitive context

GNC Mega Men Vitapak.

The offer + subscription

On Amazon, Mega Men Whole Body Vitapak is $89.99 for 30 servings, with "Extra 5% off when you subscribe" and "1K+ bought in past month." Basically BULT's subscription price with a tiny sub discount. (gnc.com blocked our browser, so this is Amazon only.)

The play worth borrowing

Being where people already shop. The vitapak lineup is split by life stage (Whole Body, 50 Plus, 40+, Performance), so guys pick themselves into one fast. The active-US exact-phrase Meta search for "gnc.com" shows about 87 results, not a verified advertiser total.

How BULT beats it

Trust and service. GNC's company-level Trustpilot profile sits at 1.5 (270 reviews), and that review sample's summary calls out customer service and delivery problems. This is not a product rating or a representative survey of all customers. BULT can make service a reason to choose it: a clean guarantee, a real human on email, and fixed policy pages (see findings 01 and 02).

The short version

Onnit wins on reviews and one loud hook. Persona wins on human help and naming the friction. GNC wins on shelf presence; its reviewed service complaints suggest an opportunity for BULT. BULT can take the pouch story from Onnit, the expert and friction angle from Persona, and the service opportunity suggested by GNC's review sample.

03 / Recommendations

A retention-first path
to a steady 10k.

The plain-words math.

A steady 10k means the number of new subs coming in each month matches the number cancelling. So one key lever is churn, meaning the share of subs who cancel each month. Acquisition is still needed to reach the target.

Example numbers only, not BULT's real data:

  • At 10% monthly churn, holding 10,000 subs takes about 1,000 new subs every month.
  • At 6% churn, it's about 600 a month.
  • Same 10k, 40% fewer replacement starts—and 40% less replacement-acquisition spend at the same CAC—just from people staying longer.

On the money side, here's the simple version. CAC is what it costs on average to get one new sub (ad spend divided by new subs). An average sub stays roughly 1 / churn months. Example: at 8% churn, that's about 12.5 months. Using the $89 recurring every-30-days price as a simplified monthly proxy, that's about $1,100 in revenue per sub before product cost. This excludes the $69 first-shipment offer. Your CAC has to sit comfortably under what that sub earns you after product and shipping.

Question for Alex: we don't know BULT's CAC yet. Do you know what a new sub costs you right now, and what monthly churn looks like? Those two numbers, alongside margin and cash payback, help shape the plan.

04 / Acquisition + retention

What 10k takes.

CAC in plain words.

CAC (customer acquisition cost) is what it costs you on average to get one new subscriber. Total ad and promo spend for a month, divided by new subs that month. Spend $10,000 and get 100 new subs, and your CAC is $100.

It only means something next to how much a sub is worth over their whole time with you. That's LTV (lifetime value).

Worked example.

EVERY NUMBER BELOW IS AN EXAMPLE, NOT BULT DATA. Only the $89 recurring every-30-days price is real (bult.com, Oct 24, 2026), used here as a simplified monthly proxy. The $69 first-shipment offer is excluded. We don't know BULT's churn, margin or CAC yet.

Worked-example inputs
InputValue
Price$89 / 30 days (real; monthly proxy)
Gross marginEXAMPLE 70%
Monthly churnEXAMPLE 8%
Target active subs10,000

  1. 1. New subs needed each month to hold 10k

    10,000 x 8% = 800 new subs a month, just to replace the ones who cancel.

  2. 2. LTV

    $89 x 70% / 8% = about $779 gross profit per sub over their lifetime (8% churn means the average sub stays about 12.5 months).

  3. 3. CAC at an illustrative 3:1 ratio

    Using an illustrative LTV:CAC target of 3:1: $779 / 3 = about $260 per new sub. This is a scenario ratio ceiling, not a universally safe or affordable maximum; actual affordability depends on payback, cash flow and costs outside gross margin.

  4. 4. Ad budget that implies

    800 x $260 = about $208,000 a month at that scenario ratio ceiling. If the real CAC is $130, it's about $104,000.

  5. 5. Why churn is the big lever (still EXAMPLE)

    Drop churn from 8% to 5% and you only need 500 new subs a month. LTV goes up to about $1,246, and CAC at the illustrative 3:1 ratio goes up to about $415. At the same actual CAC of $130, the replacement-acquisition budget falls from $104,000 to $65,000. If spending at each scenario's ratio ceiling instead, the hold budget stays roughly $208,000; lower churn does not reduce it.

  6. 6. Getting there from where you are now (EXAMPLE)

    Starting at 500 subs and adding 1,500 new subs a month at 8% churn, you'd cross 10,000 in about 9 months. Once you're there, you drop to 800 a month to hold it.
